# Miter corner cam jig

A small jig that squares one mitered frame corner, pulls the miter closed, and holds it while you mark and drill for the mending plate. You use the same jig on each corner in turn.

**Printable template:** [`miter-corner-cam-jig-template.pdf`](miter-corner-cam-jig-template.pdf), 3 Letter pages. Print it at 100% ("Actual size"). The 1" square on sheet 1 has to measure exactly 1".

| Sheet | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1 | Calibration square, plus the two cams at full size |
| 2 | Full-size corner layout for setting the fences and the relief hole |
| 3 | Plan view with dimensions, cut list, and notes on the gusset pocket |

## How it works

```
          ┃░░┃ piece B
          ┃░░┃      (cam B)
 fence B  ┃░░┃   ◯═══
          ┃░░┃
          ┃░░┃            (cam A)
          ┃░░╲         ═══◯
          ┃░░░╲━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  piece A
          ┗━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━  fence A
```

* The two **fences** form an outside 90° corner, and that corner sets the frame square.
* Each **cam** is an eccentric plywood disk on a screw pivot. When you swing its handle *away* from the corner, the disk presses the piece into its fence. The sandpaper on the grip edge also drags the piece along its length toward the corner. Cam A seats piece A, and cam B then drives piece B's miter against it, so the joint closes under load.
* Cam B is the same part as cam A, installed upside-down. The flipped cam is a mirror image, so it also pulls toward the corner.
* Each cam has 3/4" of travel (disk R 1-1/2", pivot offset 3/8"). The cams lock where you leave them.
* The cams are 1/2" thick and sit about 7-1/2" out from the corner. They stay below the top face of the frame and clear of the plate area, so the plate lies flat and the drill can reach every hole.

## Build

1. **Base:** cut a 14" × 14" square of flat 3/4" plywood.
2. **Fences:** cut 1-1/2"-wide strips, 14" and 12-1/2" long. Make them no taller than the frame stock, and tall enough to bear on the full-width part of the edge above any rabbet. Glue and screw them along two edges, using sheet 2 or a framing square to set them at exactly 90°. Wax the inside faces.
3. **Relief hole:** drill a 1/4" hole straight down at the inside corner of the fences. Dust and tear-out then have somewhere to go, so the miter points can seat fully in the corner.
4. **Cams:** spray-mount sheet 1 to 1/2" Baltic birch, drill the 1/4" pivot holes, cut out the cams, and sand to the line. Stick 80-grit PSA sandpaper around the red grip edge.
5. **Locate the pivots by dry-fit:** set two frame pieces in the jig with the miter closed. Place cam A about 7-1/2" from the corner, handle pointing toward the corner (the start position), with its disk just touching piece A. Drive a #10 × 1-1/4" pan-head screw with a fender washer through the pivot, snug but free to turn. Do the same for cam B, flipped face-down, on piece B.
6. **Optional, if a plywood gusset is already on the underside:** trace it on the base with a corner in place, then cut that pocket out so the frame lies flat.

## Use (each corner)

1. Lay both pieces in against the fences and slide them into the corner by hand.
2. Swing **cam A** away from the corner until it locks. Piece A is now seated in the corner.
3. Swing **cam B** the same way. It drives piece B's miter tight against piece A.
4. Check the joint. For long pieces, support the far ends level with the jig (your totes work) so they don't lever the corner up. An F-clamp across the piece and the base edge also holds it down.
5. Set the mending plate in place and mark through its holes with a self-centering (Vix) bit or a transfer punch. Drill the pilots with a depth stop so they don't go through the stock.
6. Release **cam B first**, then cam A.
